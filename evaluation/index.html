CONCEPT PREVIEW · READ-ONLY BY DESIGN

SEVEN STAGES

A defensible go/no-go.

01

Qualify the district

Identify the budget owner, operator, data owner and crew supervisor. Select one module and discuss a conditional paid scope.

02

Agree the permissions

Obtain procurement, legal, data-sharing and AMI licensing permission. Define fields, access, retention and deletion before real records.

03

Pre-register the comparison

Agree the historical baseline, evaluator, visit budget K, observation window, positive/negative samples and failure threshold.

04

Blind-rank the cases

Hide known outcomes from the ranking reviewer. Compare repaired leaks and representative false alerts at the same visit budget.

05

Review the disagreements

Inspect discordant cases, unresolved outcomes, exclusions and verification burden. Do not silently remove difficult negatives.

06

Run a paid field validation

Proceed only with signed sponsor and crew-lead approval, paid SOW and authorized AMI/pressure sharing. Utility crews own field activity.

07

Make the go/no-go decision

Independently review precommitted criteria. Pause if permissions, safety sign-off, the comparator or representative negatives are missing.

PRECOMMITTED SCORECARD

Agree the standard before the score.

MeasureDefinitionCurrent status
Precision at KField-verified leaks among top-K investigated candidates; denominator rule pre-agreedNot evaluated
Avoided non-leak visitsMatched retrospective comparison, separated from future field outcomesNot evaluated
Representative negativesLocal false alerts, sample definition and exclusionsNot agreed
Verification burdenCrew time, review time and cost per adjudicated candidateNot agreed
Unresolved outcomesCount, reasons and pre-agreed missing-data treatmentNot agreed
Paid commitmentDistinct qualified budget owners with a signed paid SOWNone demonstrated
Permission readinessNamed owner, approved dataset, procurement/legal and AMI licensesNone demonstrated
STOP CONDITIONS

Pause when the foundations are missing.

No permission. No safety sign-off. No historical comparator. No representative negatives. No agreed verification cost.

Marketing a suspected alert as a verified leak is an automatic failure. A tiny retrospective study does not establish general detection performance.
