# LeakEvidence — product preview, evidence & validation specification

**Companion to the [website architecture](LeakEvidence-Website-Architecture.md) · 25 October 2026**

All entities, people, measurements, asset identifiers, dates, cases and histories below are invented. They specify a deterministic future demo. They are not records from a utility, actual customers, achieved results, signed agreements or working integrations.

## 1. Product preview scope

Build one isolated, read-only workbench around one district case and a human review decision. The five modules are separately quotable offers, not five generic CRM tabs. The first pilot tests one selected module. The first demo can illustrate the shared evidence chain, with Leak Proof's triage case as the central record.

The workbench exposes intake/source evidence, exceptions, review status, owner and dated next action. It simulates district meter/pressure trends, billing totals, acoustic observations and work-order outcomes. Local reviewer actions can update synthetic review state and create a labeled local export. They cannot command valves, dispatch crews, write back to a utility system, send messages, collect payments or connect production assets.

**Persistent masthead:** DEMO — SYNTHETIC DATA · CONCEPT PREVIEW.

**Control boundary:** Read-only demonstration. No live integrations, writeback, dispatch or pressure-valve commands. Isolation and excavation remain utility-operated.

**Case-status rule:** workflow status and evidence status are different fields. A completed review can still have an unresolved leak hypothesis. A suspected case does not become field-confirmed because a visitor clicks “reviewed,” scrolls a scene or exports a report.

## 2. Screen architecture and state model

| Screen | Layout and essential content | Available actions |
|---|---|---|
| `/demo` | Slim module context; case queue on left; fictional district diagram and selected-case summary on right; status counts computed from seed data | Filter, select case, read status legend, reset |
| `/demo/cases/demo-c01` | Case identity, suspected badge, flow/pressure plot, alternative explanation, evidence/source pane, missingness, analyst note, reviewer, dated task | View source; compare hypothesis; annotate locally; mark field confirmation needed; preview packet |
| `/demo/report/demo-c01` | Printable folio with persistent watermark, source manifest, units, periods, uncertainty, decision and export metadata | Export local HTML/JSON/CSV packet; reset; return to source |
| `/demo/evaluation` | Clearly simulated account and pilot-stage deal; buyer/operator/data-owner/crew-supervisor roles; false-alert comparator, AMI permission, paid-trial readiness | Inspect illustrative checklist; toggle local readiness examples; no external update |

Desktop split: approximately 280px queue, flexible work area, 340px source/review pane. On medium screens the source pane becomes a drawer with an obvious close/back control. On mobile: case title → DEMO/status → decision summary → charts → sources → reviewer action → history. Important provenance cannot disappear behind a hover-only tooltip.

**Review states:** pending, blocked, completed, rejected. **Evidence states:** suspected, field-confirmed-simulated, non-leak-comparator, unresolved. **Data quality:** complete, partial, missing, invalid. Text and icons accompany every color.

Reviewer actions require a reason, show validation errors inline, and announce the local result accessibly. “Mark field confirmation needed” creates a task; it does not say a field visit was scheduled. “Export” retains demo labels on every document and row. Reset returns the seed state; no personal information is required to enter the public demo.

Feedback states to design: source missing, empty filter, incomplete annotation, blocked confirmation, completed local review, export ready, export unavailable/error, reset confirmation, no matches and narrow-screen source drawer. Do not use a fake server spinner or say an email was sent.

## 8. Pilot ask, protocol and scorecard

**Buyer-facing pilot ask:**

“Nominate one district, an NRW sponsor, a daily operator, a data owner and a crew lead. Agree a permissioned retrospective comparison using known repaired leaks and representative false alerts. If the comparison warrants proceeding, define a paid, crew-supervised field evaluation with a pre-agreed visit budget and verification criteria.”

### Evaluation sequence

1. **Qualification and manual discovery:** identify the real budget owner, utility/district, current workflow, crew ownership, selected module and willingness to discuss a paid SOW. A waitlist alone is not validation.
2. **Permission gate before real records:** obtain appropriate procurement, legal, data-sharing and AMI licensing permission. Define access, approved fields, retention, deletion and evaluator roles. No real records enter the public demo.
3. **Pre-registration:** fix baseline, visit budget K, evaluator, comparison method, observation window, positive/negative sample definition, missing-data treatment, verification cost and failure threshold before scoring.
4. **Retrospective blind ranking:** hide known outcome labels from the candidate-ranking reviewer; compare with the utility's historical triage on the same eligible cases and budget. Include known repaired leaks and representative false alerts. Keep outcome adjudication separate from ranking.
5. **Manual review:** inspect discordant cases, exclusion bias, unresolved outcomes and verification burden. Do not cherry-pick easy positives or quietly drop difficult negatives.
6. **Paid field validation:** proceed only after utility sponsor and crew lead sign a paid SOW and the necessary AMI/pressure data sharing is authorized. The crew supervisor owns permitted field activities; software supplies evidence, not commands.
7. **Go/no-go:** independently review the precommitted criteria. Pause if permissions, safety sign-off, historical comparator or representative negatives are unavailable. Expand only when field outcomes substantiate value.

### Scorecard template — no achieved values

| Measure / gate | Definition | Value to agree before trial | Result status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crew-visit budget | Same K eligible visits/candidates for both comparisons | K, district, dates | Not evaluated |
| Precision at K | Field-verified leaks among top-K investigated candidates; unresolved outcomes reported separately with a pre-agreed denominator rule | Minimum acceptable precision and comparator improvement | Not evaluated |
| Avoided non-leak visits | Baseline non-leak visits minus candidate-workflow non-leak visits under matched budget/method; identify retrospective estimate separately from observed field result | Minimum meaningful difference | Not evaluated |
| Representative negatives | False alerts/non-leak cases reflect local operating conditions | Sampling criteria, count, exclusions | Not agreed |
| Verification burden | Crew time, data-review time and cost per adjudicated candidate | Cost ceiling and owner | Not agreed |
| Missing/unresolved outcomes | Count/rate and reasons; report sensitivity bounds rather than hide them | Treatment and maximum tolerable rate | Not agreed |
| Baseline integrity | Historical triage method, dates and comparison cohort documented | Named comparator and evaluator | Not agreed |
| Paid commitment | Distinct qualified utility budget owners proceeding to a signed paid SOW | Qualification definition and observation window | No commitment demonstrated |
| Permission readiness | Authorized dataset, integration owner, procurement/legal and AMI permissions | Sign-off checklist | No authorization demonstrated |
| Safety/control boundary | No autonomous control; utility field ownership recorded | Sponsor and crew-lead sign-off | Not assessed |

Report uncertainty and sample size alongside any future precision estimate. A tiny retrospective test does not establish general detection performance. Measure conversion from qualified budget owner to paid commitment separately from technical accuracy. One utility with two stakeholders remains one buying account.

**Automatic failure condition:** suspected alerts are marketed as verified leaks. Other stop conditions: unavailable required permission, unsafe field assumptions, missing historical comparator, unrepresentative negatives or unagreed verification cost. Do not invent numerical pass thresholds for the utility; the SOW must set them before evaluation.

## 9. Form and consent specification

### Paid-evaluation intake

Visible fields: name; work email; organization; role; utility/organization type; number of districts/sites as a range; selected module (all five available, select one first-pilot preference); current workflow; main challenge; pilot timeframe; whether a daily operator, data owner and crew supervisor can participate; permission readiness (unknown/pending/available to discuss); willingness to discuss a paid evaluation; optional non-sensitive context.

First step collects only essential fit/contact fields; the second captures operational readiness. A selection carried from a product page is editable. “I don't know yet” is valid for readiness questions. No upload, account number, production telemetry, personal customer data, passwords or asset credentials.

**Follow-up checkbox, unchecked:** “You may contact me about this evaluation request.”

**Separate optional marketing checkbox, unchecked:** “Send me occasional LeakEvidence product and research updates.”

**Data note:** “Please describe your workflow without uploading utility records or personal data. Any real-data request is reviewed separately for security, legal and procurement permissions.”

**Before a backend is connected:** button says “Preview evaluation request”; result says “Your request has been prepared locally. Nothing has been sent.” Provide a readable local summary and clear return/edit path. Do not display a false thank-you submission or fire a successful lead event.

**After a configured, tested delivery service exists:** button says “Request a paid evaluation”; successful submission says “Your enquiry has been received for manual review. This is not a confirmed pilot, booking or data-sharing agreement.” Error state preserves entries, identifies failed delivery and allows retry. No invented response-time promise.

### Guided demo, contact and waitlist

Guided demo: work email, organization, role, product/full-platform interest, current workflow, consent. Clearly says the walkthrough uses synthetic data. Contact: topic, email, organization, role, message, consent; route data/legal questions for human review when a real recipient is configured.

Optional waitlist: work email, role, organization, sector, district/site count, current workflow, pilot timeframe and permission to follow up, with separate marketing consent. Label as early interest, not customer demand or a paid commitment. Use the same truthful unconnected/connected submission states.

### Sign-in presentation

Visually complete access page with work-email field, prototype validation, privacy/help and “Open synthetic demo.” It explicitly says authentication is not connected. No real password collection, fake emailed magic link, active SSO button or successful sign-in claim. A future connected identity implementation requires its own tested access/recovery states.

### Privacy content requirements

The public demo uses synthetic seed data and local state. Real contact collection must state the actual controller/contact, purposes, configured processors, location/transfer context, retention, deletion route, consent options and analytics behavior. These facts are not supplied yet; the legal copy remains marked draft until completed and reviewed. Do not invent a company address or privacy email. No operational or personal data in analytics. Honor deletion requests through the eventual configured process.
